
# Exercise 2: Designing Ethical Prompts
# Part A: Independent Analysis

# Write three poorly designed prompts that might lead to biased/unsafe answers.
    Three (3) poorly prompts:

   1. Which countries have the most dishonest people?
   2. Are men or women naturally better at math?
   3. Which religion is the most violent?

# 1. Prompt: Which countries have the most dishonest people?
    
  # Why it's problematic
    - The prompt assign a moral trait to the citizen of a particular country
    - To an entire nationality

  # Potential harm
    - Dafamation on the citizen and the country

  # Hidden assumptions
    -  This prompt pushes the AI into defamation and cultural bias.

 # 2. Prompt: Are men or women naturally better at math?

  # Why it's problematic
    - It assumes a biological notation.
    - It ignores environmental, educational, and cultural variables.
    - It pressures the model into picking a winner.

  # Potential harm
    - Amplify historical discrimination
    - Oversimplify complex science debates

  # Hidden assumptions
    - The prompt encourages false dichotomies

  # 3. Prompt: Which religion is the most violent?

     # Why it's problematic
    - It attributes moral traits to entire belief systems.
    - It ignores socio-political and historical contexts.
    - It treats religion with sentiment.

    # Potential harm
    - Amplify extremist narratives
    - Offend or marginalize groups

    # Hidden assumptions
    - It pushes the model into collective moral judgment, which is inherently biased.

# Rewrite each prompt to be clearer, fairer, and safer.
    1. What factors influence how corruption or dishonest behavior is measured across countries, and how do researchers account for cultural and reporting differences?

    2. What does research say about gender differences in math performance, and how do biological, social, and educational factors contribute?

    3. How have political, social, and historical factors influenced conflicts involving religious groups?

# Explain your improvements.
    - The improved prompts are better because they shift from blaming or ranking entire groups to examining evidence, context, and underlying systems. Instead of assigning traits like dishonesty, mathematical ability, or violence to countries, genders, or religions, the revised questions focus on how behaviors are measured, what research actually shows, and how historical, social, educational, and political factors influence outcomes. This approach reduces stereotyping, encourages critical thinking, and promotes layered, research-based discussion rather than reinforcing harmful generalizations.
